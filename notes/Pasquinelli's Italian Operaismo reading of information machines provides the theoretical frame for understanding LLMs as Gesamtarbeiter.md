# Pasquinelli's Italian Operaismo reading of information machines provides the theoretical frame for understanding LLMs as Gesamtarbeiter

Italian Operaismo (workerism) was a political and theoretical tradition that emerged in Italy in the 1960s-70s, associated with figures like Antonio Negri, Mario Tronti, Romano Alquati, and later Paolo Virno. Its analytical core was a close reading of Marx's Capital from the standpoint of the factory floor: the composition of the working class, the technical and political composition of labor, the cycle of accumulation and struggle. Operaismo took seriously the idea that the organization of production is a site of political conflict, not merely an economic background.

Matteo Pasquinelli's 2015 paper "Italian Operaismo and the Information Machine" applies this tradition to information technology. The key move: information machines — from Turing machines through computers to the internet — are not a break from industrial machinery but a continuous expression of the same historical tendency. They crystallize social knowledge (what Marx called general intellect) in a new medium. Information machines absorb cognitive labor in the same way that industrial machinery absorbed manual labor: by decomposing it, systematizing it, and embedding it in fixed capital.

This framework is what makes LLMs legible as Gesamtarbeiter — collective workers — rather than as tools, oracles, or autonomous agents. The LLM is not a mind thinking; it is the crystallized labor of millions of thinkers, organized by capital, deployed as productive apparatus. The Gesamtarbeiter framing insists that we see the social labor behind the machine output, just as operaismo insisted that we see the social labor behind the manufactured commodity.

What the operaismo tradition adds that pure Marxist theory sometimes lacks: attention to the specificity of cognitive labor, the information content of production, and the political composition of knowledge workers as a class. LLMs as information machines demand this specificity.
