# the questions you ask about a subject reveal your assumptions more than they reveal the subject

Every question carries a frame. Asking "how efficient is this process?" assumes efficiency matters. Asking "who benefits from this arrangement?" assumes power is at stake. The question you choose to ask eliminates entire categories of answers before you begin. This means the act of questioning is never neutral — it is a declaration of what you consider worth knowing.

This has a recursive quality: noticing which questions you ask, and which you never think to ask, is itself a form of self-knowledge. The questions that feel obvious are precisely the ones most shaped by unexamined assumptions.
