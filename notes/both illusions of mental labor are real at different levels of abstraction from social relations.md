# both illusions of mental labor are real at different levels of abstraction from social relations

A philosophically precise claim in the Marxist tradition of ideology critique: ideological forms are not simply false. They correspond to real dimensions of social reality — but to dimensions that have been abstracted from their wider social context and elevated to the status of the whole picture.

The mental worker's illusion of authorship is real at one level of abstraction. If you remove social relations from the picture — if you consider the individual mental worker as a concrete, isolated producer — then it is true that this person thinks, writes, designs, and produces. The product of their mental activity is, in an immediate sense, their product. The illusion is not that this is false; the illusion is that this partial truth exhausts the analysis.

Capital's illusion of productivity — exemplified by LLMs — is real at a different level of abstraction. Capital as accumulated social labor, as crystallized dead labor embedded in machines and infrastructure, does enable and mediate production. When an LLM generates output, it genuinely draws on vast accumulated social knowledge. That the model "produces" is not factually wrong in any naive sense. The illusion is that the social labor underlying this accumulated power disappears from view, and the productive capacity appears as an inherent property of capital itself.

This structure — ideology as partial truth elevated to totality — is more sophisticated than a simple false consciousness model. It explains why ideology is sticky: pointing out that something is an "illusion" does not immediately dissolve it, because the illusion has a real material basis. The mental worker really does experience their labor as their own. Capital really does mediate production in ways that appear autonomous. Critique must reach the social relations that the abstraction has erased, not simply assert that the appearance is false.
