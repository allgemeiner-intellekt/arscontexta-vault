# iterative AI interaction clarifies the human's thinking not just the AI's output

When you revise a prompt five or six times, something happens beyond prompt improvement. Each revision requires you to diagnose what was wrong — what information was missing, what constraint you had left implicit, what assumption the AI could not have known to share. This diagnosis is a form of thinking. By the fifth revision, you have articulated the task more precisely than you could have before the first attempt.

This is the same mechanism as teaching. Explaining something to someone who doesn't share your background forces you to make explicit what you assumed was obvious. The AI's lack of context serves a similar function: it makes your unstated assumptions visible by failing to fulfill them.

The outcome is bidirectional: the AI's response improves through iteration, but so does the user's understanding of their own task. Prompt engineering, practiced iteratively, is a method for achieving conceptual clarity — not just a method for getting better AI output.
