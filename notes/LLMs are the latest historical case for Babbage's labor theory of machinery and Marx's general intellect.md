# LLMs are the latest historical case for Babbage's labor theory of machinery and Marx's general intellect

Charles Babbage analyzed machinery from the standpoint of labor's decomposition: machines embody the skill and knowledge of workers in a form that can be reproduced, scaled, and operated without the original skilled worker. The machinery crystallizes what was once tacit and personal into mechanical form. This is Babbage's labor theory of machinery — machinery as congealed labor-knowledge.

Marx extended this in the Grundrisse's "Fragment on Machines": the concept of general intellect (allgemeiner Intellekt). As science and knowledge become productive forces, they are no longer just properties of individual workers but of society as a whole — embedded in machinery, organization, and infrastructure. Fixed capital absorbs social knowledge and deploys it independently of any individual worker's participation. The machine thinks and operates using the accumulated knowledge of the collective.

LLMs are the most vivid contemporary case for both frameworks. They embody Babbage's mechanism at a linguistic level: the skills of writers, reasoners, and coders — previously tacit and distributed — are decomposed, extracted, and crystallized into parameters. They instantiate Marx's general intellect at an unprecedented scale: the accumulated linguistic and cognitive labor of millions of people across decades of internet content becomes fixed capital, operable as a productive machine.

What LLMs add to the theoretical picture is a domain that was previously difficult to reach: language and cognition. Earlier machinery addressed physical manipulation and calculation. LLMs extend fixed-capital absorption to language, argument, and knowledge-production. Pasquinelli's operaismo frame provides the bridge: information machines are not merely analogous to industrial machinery but are continuous with it as a historical form.
