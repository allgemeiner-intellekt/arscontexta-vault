# recurring disagreements often stem from different frames rather than different facts

Two people can look at the same situation, agree on every fact, and still disagree — because they are answering different questions. One asks "what is fair?" while the other asks "what works?" The disagreement feels irresolvable because neither party realizes they are operating in different frames. The conflict is not about the answer but about which question matters.

Noticing this pattern requires stepping outside the content of the disagreement and observing its structure. This is difficult in the moment precisely because the frame feels invisible to the person inside it.
